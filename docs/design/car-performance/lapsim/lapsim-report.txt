# RAN WHEN PARKED - lap-time model, all cars (external review, prototype)

## The model
Lap time = quasi-static point-mass sim over a course of corners + straights.
 - corner apex speed = sqrt(mu * g * radius), mu from the signed-off grip model
 - straights: accelerate on the car's OWN MEASURED acceleration curve, a(v) = min(aLaunch,
   pEff / m v) - drag, whose two unknowns are SOLVED from that car's published 0-97
   and 0-161 km/h; 59 of the 85 have both, 4 have one, the rest fall back to a
   regression on the 59. Aero drag = Cd x frontal area (0.82 x width x height), speed
   capped at the published top speed, then braking into the next corner on the car's
   OWN measured braking coefficient bmu, which is not its lateral mu (see below)
 - pEff governs the range it was MEASURED over and no more. Above 161 km/h the share of
   the shortfall that the car's own arithmetic says was traction rather than engine is
   handed back, completing at the car's top speed, which is where the model already
   assumed full crank power. It fires on 3 of the 85 and is exactly zero on the rest;
   nothing below 161 km/h moves, so every measured car still round-trips its own pair
 - a DIRECTION-CHANGE CHARGE, kAgi / mu x (angle/90) x a tightness clamp, added in
   SECONDS once per corner. THIS IS THE TERM THE PUBLISHED TABLE RUNS. Its weight is
   fitted on ALL driven laps on ALL THREE courses at once, equal weight per course
   (stderr section 2b)
 - a CORNER-EXIT SPEED PENALTY, built and fitted beside it and SWITCHED OFF: kExit is
   0 in the published run, which makes the term return exactly zero. It is the more
   honest shape, and that is why it exists: a car would leave a direction change
   carrying less speed than the apex formula gives it, and the following straight
   would START from the reduced speed, so a real deficit would propagate down the road
   instead of being a flat per-corner charge blind to what follows. It is off because
   at its own best weight it costs level accuracy on all three lap courses: both
   facsimile geometries were searched under the additive term and are held FIXED, so
   the roads have already absorbed what the adder got wrong about them. The whole
   comparison is kept and scored, because it is the evidence (stderr section 2c)
 - KNOWN STRUCTURAL GAP, AND WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT, stated up front: on the SURVEYED
   Hakone geometry the model is about 21% slow on every driven lap even with the
   direction-change term switched off, and no direction-change term repairs it. The
   missing physics is a racing-line model - a mapped radius is a centreline radius -
   and it is not a gradient, which prices out at a fraction of the gap. The model has
   NOT been given that term. Instead, on maintainer ruling (2026-07-27), the Hakone
   course is now a BEHAVIOURAL FACSIMILE: a 2.7 km road searched to reproduce the
   first eight driven times, not a survey of the real one. Read every Hakone number on
   that footing. The surveyed map is kept in the file and every diagnostic scores it.
 - AND ONE MORE CAVEAT ON THAT COMPARISON: both facsimile geometries were searched
   with the additive term in place and are held FIXED while the exit term is fitted,
   by instruction, so the two are scored on the same roads. That handicaps the exit
   term, and the size of the handicap is in stderr 2c: the frozen Hakone road needs
   about 14% of a lap from its direction-change term, which an exit-speed deficit
   structurally cannot supply without saturating.
 - the ranked table below runs at the constants THIS RUN FITS, printed above it, not at any
   value frozen in the source. Frontal areas are REAL (0.82 x width x height) for
   all 85 cars from published dimensions.
 - the 64 cars Forza Horizon 6 carries ARE their Forza entries here, Forza's name and year
   included; the other 21 are web-sourced and modelled.
 - the standing kilometre is now a COURSE, Yatabe Straight, and it carries the only
   calibration constant in the model that is not physics. Seven measured 1 km runs
   exist and the raw model is slow on every one of them, by +1.3% to +6.4%, mean
   +3.4%, in an order that neither power nor power-to-weight predicts. Five candidate
   mechanisms were priced against those points and none survived, so on maintainer
   ruling (2026-07-27) a FLAT PROTOCOL OFFSET is subtracted from computed 1 km times
   and from nothing else. It is not physics: the model is fed Forza's canned panel
   figures and the driven runs are hand-driven with the assists off, which are
   plausibly not the same measurement. It reaches the drag strip ONLY - see the
   DRAG_OFFSET comment for why touching the lap path would break three courses.

## The 4 courses
- Hakone: 2.7 km, 9 corners (5 medium, 4 hairpin), 80% straight, avg radius 63 m, longest straight 290 m
- Wangan: 7.0 km, 11 corners (8 fast, 2 medium, 1 slow), 77% straight, avg radius 355 m, longest straight 838 m
- Misaki: 4.7 km, 10 corners (6 fast, 2 medium, 1 slow, 1 hairpin), 68% straight, avg radius 285 m, longest straight 650 m
- Yatabe: 1.0 km in a straight line from a standing start, no corners
  Hakone = a 2.7 km mountain-road FACSIMILE, searched against the FIRST 8 driven laps
           on it, NOT a survey: four 11 m switchbacks and five linking bends -> grip
           and direction change. The surveyed road (eleven 11-19 m switchbacks) is in
           the file as HAK_MAP; nothing gets the model closer than ~21% slow on it,
           and that floor is the direction-change term switched OFF.
  Wangan = a 7.0 km highway loop FACSIMILE, searched against the FIRST 5 driven laps:
           eight fast sweepers of 295-645 m that are straights for a slow car, two
           mediums, one 55 m junction ramp -> drag, power and grip ABOVE 161 km/h,
           which is where the measured acceleration curve stops being measured.
           The description it was authored from is in the file as WAN_DESC; on that
           reading the model runs FAST and the search had to add corner demand.
  Misaki = the calibrated Legend Island facsimile; 17 driven laps are on it, and it is
           the only one of the three lap courses whose geometry was never tuned to a
           lap time.
  Yatabe = 1 km from rest in a straight line, no corners: the JARI proving ground
           name for the standing-kilometre set that has been scored since 2026-07-27
           and is a course from this run on. It measures acceleration and drag with
           the cornering model taken out of the question entirely, and it is the only
           course carrying a calibration offset (DRAG_OFFSET, drag strip only).
  The corner-exit weight is fitted on the Misaki, Hakone and Wangan driven laps
  TOGETHER, weighted equally by course; Yatabe is in NO fit of any lap constant.
  EVERY course here carries driven times: the synthetic club Circuit was dropped on
  maintainer ruling (2026-07-27) and the overall index is the mean over these four,
  at equal weight per course. READ THE INDEX WITH THAT IN MIND: a quarter of it is now
  a single straight-line measure, and the ranked table below prints what that moved.
  THREE CARS ARE OUT OF SAMPLE FOR ALL OF IT. The 190E Evo II, the Civic Type R EK9 and
  the Impreza 22B were predicted on all three courses before a lap of any of them was
  driven, so neither searched geometry nor any fitted weight had ever seen them. Their
  committed predictions are printed beside their driven times in every table below.

## Braking grip is a separate measurement from lateral grip
bmu is solved from each measured stopping distance with the aero-corrected integral
  bmu = ln(1 + k V^2) / (2 g k (d - d0)),  k = dfC * 6.2e-5
d0 is the dead distance every published stop carries in front of the retardation.
The 97-0 solution is the stored value; the 161-0 solution is the check on it.

### The dead distance d0
  Solved per car (two equations, two unknowns) over the 59 cars with both figures:
    min 3.10  p25 5.15  median 6.19  p75 7.12  max 10.87 m   (mean 6.18, sd 1.53)
  GLOBAL CONSTANT: least squares on the two-point disagreement gives d0 = 5.987 m, i.e. 0.222 s of
  brake application at 97 km/h. The median of the 59 is 6.192 m: the two differ by 0.21 m and their residual rms by 0.024 points.
  Least squares is the one used, on two grounds: the per-car spread is near-symmetric (mean
  and median agree to 0.01 m) so the median buys no robustness here, and least squares minimises the
  disagreement the constant exists to remove rather than a proxy for it.

  two-point disagreement, bmu161 against bmu97 (n=59):
    d0 = 0 (before) min   4.98  p25   8.12  median   9.86  p75  10.85  max  14.01   mean   9.59  MAE 9.59  rms 9.79  positive 59/59
    d0 = 5.99 m     min  -5.10  p25  -1.44  median   0.32  p75   1.83  max   6.52   mean   0.18  MAE 1.94  rms 2.43  positive 31/59
  VERDICT: the gap was one-signed on all 59 cars and is now split 31/28, with the mean bias down from 9.59% to 0.18%.
  It has collapsed, not vanished: 2.43% rms remains and it is unsigned. That is the size a per-car spread in brake-application
  rate plus 0.005-g panel rounding on the lateral pair (which moves dfC, hence k) would leave.

## CONSTANTS THIS TABLE RUNS AT
   kAgi 0.8170 (additive direction-change term)  kExit 0 [the corner-exit penalty is scored at 94.5833 tau p 1.0 q 1.0 a 1.5 t 1.5 hi 2.5 lo 0 and NOT applied]  brake d0 5.987 m  corner-grip ceiling 1.220 at 20 m, exponent 0.0612   (acceleration: measured on 59 cars, 4 one-point, 22 regressed)
   Acceleration is no longer a constant. phi, awdK, aCapK and the delivery archetype table
   are gone from the published model: each car's launch acceleration and effective wheel
   power are SOLVED from its own published 0-97 and 0-161, and the cars Forza does not
   measure fall back to a regression on the 59 that it does. d0 is least squares on the 59
   two-point braking measurements. kAgi is fitted on ALL 38 driven laps, 17 on Misaki, 12 on
   Hakone and 9 on Wangan, weighted equally BY COURSE rather than by lap. The corner-exit
   penalty is fitted on the same 38 and scored beside it; the two sweeps are stderr 2b and 2c.
   THE DIRECTION-CHANGE TERM THIS TABLE RUNS IS THE TIME ADDER, and it is worth being plain
   about that. The model charges kAgi / mu x (angle/90) x a tightness clamp in SECONDS, once
   per corner. Its weakness is structural rather than a matter of tuning: a car it penalises
   leaves the corner at exactly the speed it would have had anyway, so the charge cannot
   propagate down the following straight, which is how a real direction-change deficit costs
   time.
   A CORNER-EXIT SPEED PENALTY IS BUILT FOR THAT AND IS NOT APPLIED. It reduces corner-EXIT
   SPEED instead of adding seconds: the car would leave a direction change carrying less speed
   than the apex formula gives it and pay for that until the road opens out. It compounds on
   hairpins onto short connectors and is worth almost nothing on a motorway sweeper, so the
   tight-versus-fast axis would come out of the arithmetic rather than a fitted constant. kExit
   is 0 in this run and the term returns exactly zero, so every figure quoted for it below is a
   score and not a setting.
   READ BOTH FITTED WEIGHTS WITH THE FACSIMILE CAVEAT. TWO of the three courses have
   searched geometries: Hakone against its first eight laps and Wangan against its first five
   (maintainer rulings, 2026-07-27), so both were chosen to suit the model - and both were
   searched with the ADDITIVE term in place and are held FIXED here, which handicaps the exit
   term and is the reason it stays off.
   On the SURVEYED Hakone map the model is about 22% slow whatever term is used. The
   Misaki third of the fit is untouched by any of this: its geometry is not a free parameter
   and never has been.
   AND THAT HANDICAP IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT, BECAUSE IT IS NOT SMALL PRINT. The frozen
   Hakone road wants about 14% of a lap from whatever direction-change term the model carries,
   and an exit-speed deficit structurally cannot supply that: to get close it has to saturate.
   At its own fitted weight the exit deficit is against its own floor on a large share of the
   corners in the driven sets, which is exactly where the term stops separating cars. Read the
   course-character swing section below for what that costs and what the smaller, non-saturating
   weight would have bought.

## THE TRACTION RELEASE ABOVE 161 KM/H
   pEff is solved from a 0-97 and a 0-161 and was then applied at EVERY speed. On a car whose
   tyres run out before its engine does, that window measures the contact patch rather than the
   engine, and holding its answer flat makes the model assert two incompatible things at once:
   a top speed only full crank power reaches, and six tenths of that power on the way there.
   THE DETECTION IS ARITHMETIC ON WHAT IS ALREADY SOLVED. vFull = Pw / (m aLaunch) is the speed
   below which full crank power asks the tyres for more thrust than the solved launch plateau
   can give; fTr is the share of the 97-161 window sitting below it. Above 161 km/h that share
   of the shortfall is handed back, completing at the car's own top speed. Below 161 nothing
   changes at all, which is why every measured car still round-trips its own 0-97 and 0-161.
   IT FIRES ON 3 OF THE 85 CARS IN THE TABLE. For the other 82, vFull is below 97 km/h, fTr is exactly zero,
   and the acceleration curve is bit-for-bit the one the previous run published.

### the one reading that fits the sign, and why the harness may not use it
  Solve each car against its published pair scaled by (1 - d) and run the kilometre
  on the result with no offset added back. d is how optimistic Forza's own 0-97 and
  0-161 would have to be for the model to land the driven time. It BREAKS both
  round trips by construction and is a diagnosis, not a candidate.
  IT NEEDS BOTH PUBLISHED TIMES. Every car in this set has both, so none is missing

### VERDICT: no mechanism, one flat offset
  Every currency the deficit has been expressed in spreads by several times across these 7 cars:
  1.3% to 6.4% as a percentage, 0.27 s to 1.47 s as a time, and 4% to 18% as an error in the
  published pair. A shared PARAMETER is a mechanism only when the cars agree on its
  value, and on this evidence they never do: every candidate above buys a mean and
  sells the Countach and the Beat, which are the two cars that test whether it is
  real. THE MODEL IS THEREFORE STILL UNCHANGED. What HAS changed, on maintainer
  ruling, is that the residual is now admitted as a calibration instead of being
  carried as a known error: a flat -3.28% on
  computed kilometres, fitted on these runs, applied to no lap on any course, and
  labelled a PROTOCOL OFFSET because that is what the evidence supports. It is not
  a term. Nothing about a car changes it, and it explains nothing.
  WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT: more standing kilometres, and at least one on a car whose
  panel 0-97 the maintainer has re-driven by hand, which is the one measurement
  that separates a protocol gap from a model defect. If that measurement ever comes
  in, the offset is the first thing that should be deleted.
  ON THE HIGH-SPEED RELEASE, which these runs were also asked to judge: it stays,
  unchanged and per-car. It is not superseded, because nothing replaced it; it is
  supported, because switching it off makes three of the set worse and none better;
  and it is not promoted to a shared weight, because of the kappa table above.

  the course whose normalised times are most spread out contributes the most
  variance to a mean of them, and a standing kilometre spreads a field of 85 cars
  wider than any lap does, because a lap gives a slow car its corners back and a
  straight line gives it nothing. A quarter of the index therefore buys more than a
  quarter of the ordering, and every bit of what it buys is power, mass and drag.
