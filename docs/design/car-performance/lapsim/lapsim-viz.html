The measured standing kilometres

Prototype for judgement, not final balance. The 64 cars Forza Horizon 6 carries are shown as Forza's own entries, name and year included, and 63 of them run Forza's own figures; the 64th (the Eunos Cosmo) is in the game with no published stats at all, so it keeps modelled numbers, and the remaining 21 have no analogue in the game and are web-sourced and modelled throughout. Cornering grip from the signed-off model; braking on a separate per-car coefficient solved from the measured 97-0 distance with the test's fixed brake-application distance taken off first; drag from real Cd x frontal area. Acceleration is measured rather than derived: each car's launch acceleration and effective wheel power are solved from its own published 0-97 and 0-161 km/h times, so the four fitted constants that used to predict acceleration from power (phi, awdK, aCapK and an engine-archetype delivery table) are gone. That solved power governs the range it was measured over and no further: above 161 km/h a car whose own arithmetic says its 0-161 was taken while the tyres, not the engine, were the limit is handed back that share of its power shortfall, completing at its top speed, which is the speed the model already assumed full crank power at. It fires on 3 of the 85 cars, moves no lap on any course by half a per cent, and leaves every measured car reproducing its own published 0-97 and 0-161 exactly. The badge on each car says which of the three states its curve is in, and hovering it gives the solved figures. Every figure here runs at the constants the calibration harness fitted, shown above, not at anything frozen in the source. "Overall" is the mean of a car's time-ratio to the fastest car on each course (1.000 = fastest everywhere), at equal weight per course.

One of those courses is a straight line, and equal weight is not equal influence. Yatabe Straight is a 1 km standing start with no corners, and it spreads the field wider than any of the three laps does, because a lap hands a slow car its corners back and a straight line hands it nothing. A course with a wider spread of normalised times contributes more variance to a mean of them, so a quarter of the index buys rather more than a quarter of the ordering, and what it buys is power, mass and drag. The harness prints the ranking with and without it, and the ranking is unchanged at the sharp end and moves cars in the middle. Yatabe is also the only course carrying a calibration constant: a flat protocol offset on computed kilometres, fitted on the driven runs, applied to no lap anywhere.

Two cars carry a provenance flag in the spec book and neither should be quoted without it. The 1989 Silvia K's has six figures overridden to the Forza wiki's stock panel, because ForzaLabs measured the game's preset starter car and not the stock one: its lateral pair, braking pair, top speed, the drag derived from that top speed, its kerb and its 0-97. Its 0-161 still has no stock counterpart, so its solved effective power remains preset-tainted while its launch figure does not. The Celica GT-Four ST205 is confirmed as a third preset starter car but nothing is changed on it, because the wiki publishes no stock measurements for it at all: an inferred number that looks measured would be worse than a measured one that is labelled suspect.
