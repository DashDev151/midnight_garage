The Car Spec Book

Real stock specs for the whole roster, and the data layer under the car performance model. 64 cars are in Forza Horizon 6 and are shown as Forza's own entries, name and year included; 63 of those run Forza's own measured figures. Tap any car for its detail, its modelled dyno curve and the whole published panel behind it.

how to read this book

One set of figures per car, and where Forza has the car those figures are Forza's: power, torque, weight, distribution, top speed, acceleration, braking and lateral g, with grip, downforce and drag derived from them. Where Forza models a different variant of a car we wanted, the Forza variant is the car in this book, named as Forza names it. The 64th Forza car, the Eunos Cosmo, is in the game with no published stats at all, so it takes Forza's identity and keeps modelled numbers. The other 21 have no analogue in the game and stay web-sourced and modelled.

The dyno curve is modelled, not measured. It is built from the peak points and shaped by engine character: turbo spool step, the sequential-twin valley of death, the VTEC cam crossover. It is not a real dyno trace. Estimated fields are marked as such.

Two cards carry an override flag and neither should be quoted without it, because on both the scrape measured one of the game's preset starter cars rather than the stock one. The 1989 Silvia K's has six figures replaced from the Forza wiki's stock panel; its 0-161 has no stock counterpart anywhere, so that one figure stays labelled as preset-tainted. The 1994 Celica GT-Four ST205 has thirteen replaced from the game's own stock panel, read first hand, so nothing on that card is tainted afterwards.

Mind the three test speeds. Acceleration and braking are quoted at 97 and 161 km/h; lateral g at 97 and 193. They are not the same pair, and reading the second lateral figure as 161 would corrupt the downforce fit, which goes as speed squared.

Curves are modelled from peak torque/power/redline, not factory dyno traces.
64 of the 85 cars are Forza Horizon 6 cars outright, name and year included; 63 of those carry its stats panel: power, torque, kerb, weight distribution, top speed, 0-97, braking and the lateral-g pair, with grip, downforce and drag derived from those. Four of the 63 cannot reach 161 km/h, so their 161 km/h runs fail upstream and read "not reached" rather than as a gap. We calibrate against Forza's measurements, so the car in the book is the car Forza measured. The remaining 21 have no analogue in the game and stay modelled.
Estimated fields are flagged individually on the card: centre-of-mass height on every car, redline on 47, drag on 22, weight distribution on 19, and a few power, kerb and 0-100 figures on the modelled entries. Staging data, not yet in the game.
Acceleration is published to 60 mph and to 100 mph and no further. There is no 0-120 mph time in the source and nothing here interpolates one: the 120 mph figure is lateral g, a cornering reading, not an acceleration.
Where a card argues from the lap harness's Hakone or Wangan driven laps, read that evidence with the caveat the harness itself carries: both geometries are behavioural facsimiles, roads searched to reproduce driven times rather than surveys. Their LEVEL error therefore measures the search and not the model, and only the 14 driven laps on Misaki, whose geometry was traced and never tuned, test the level at all. What the two facsimiles still say honestly is ordering, field spread, pair gaps, and, because a geometry charges every car through the same corners, the per-car scatter about the fitted level.
