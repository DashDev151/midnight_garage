# Factory colours: research in progress

**Status: research COMPLETE, 94 of 94 cars.** The surviving output of a research pass that was stopped part
way. Recorded because the work is sound and because the method it established is worth more than
the cars themselves.

Feeds `docs/sprints/sprint169.md`. Nothing here is authored into content yet.

## The method, which is the most useful thing here

**`gazoo.com` per-grade catalogue pages are the reliable source.** They are grade-scoped and
date-scoped, and they mark maker-standard against maker-option. They validated independently
against Honda's own archive on the DC5, against `carsensor` on the Cosmo, and against `erix7` and
`ant-llc` on the FC3S.

**Colour-family headings in the Japanese catalogues are strong shade evidence**, and often the only
evidence. They are why Nissan's Greenish Blue is filed as teal rather than blue, why Purplish Blue
is filed as purple, and why Mazda's Diamond Grey Metallic is filed under gold and is therefore a
warm grey rather than a neutral one.

**Finish suffixes are real data**: (M) metallic, (P) pearl, (3P) three-coat pearl, (PM) pearl
metallic, (TPM) triple-coat pearl metallic, Mica.

**Sources that produce plausible but wrong lists, and must not be re-mined:**

- **221616.com (Gulliver)** returned NSX and S2000 colours for a DC5 Integra, and two mutually
  contradictory Cosmo lists. Not model-specific.
- **goo-net catalogue index pages** present a used-car search filter as a factory list.
- **ant-llc** is undated across a model's whole run and its naming is unreliable, though its codes
  are usable as cross-references.
- **paintref.com** returns HTTP 403 throughout.

**US-market archives (`importarchive.com`) are explicitly not JDM.** Useful only for attaching paint
codes and shade evidence to JDM names, since codes are shared across markets.

## Honda Integra Type R (DC5)

Two lists either side of the September 2004 facelift, both confirmed against Honda's own archive.

**2001.07 to 2004.09:** Championship White (NH-0), Milano Red (R-81), Arctic Blue Pearl, Nighthawk
Black Pearl (B-92P, black with blue pearl flake), Satin Silver Metallic.

**2004.09 to 2006.09:** Championship White, Milano Red, Vivid Blue Pearl, Nighthawk Black Pearl,
Alabaster Silver Metallic.

Honda's archive names the later silver Alabaster; gazoo names it Satin. Honda's page is the factory
source. **Championship White carried no surcharge on the Type R**, where the Type S's Premium White
Pearl cost 31,500 yen extra.

**Confidence: solid.**

## Mazda RX-8 Type RS (SE3P)

The Type RS was created at the March 2008 facelift. Its list changed once, at **May 2009**, not at
the facelift as is commonly assumed.

**2008.03 to 2009.05:** Velocity Red Mica (27A, a deep red, Mazda's signature of the era), Aurora
Blue Mica (34J), Sparkling Black Mica (35N), Sunlight Silver Metallic (22V), Diamond Grey Metallic
(38E, **filed under GOLD, so a warm champagne-cast grey rather than neutral**), Crystal White Pearl
Mica (34K), Galaxy Grey Mica (32S).

**2009.05 onward:** Sunlight Silver, Diamond Grey and Galaxy Grey are dropped for Aluminium
Metallic (38P) and Metropolitan Grey Mica. Aurora Blue becomes Stormy Blue in **September 2010**.

**Confidence: solid.**

## Toyota, six cars

A second surviving pass. Same method, same source hierarchy, and it independently confirmed
`gazoo.com` as the reliable source and `221616.com` as contaminated: that site returned **BMW and
Mercedes colour names** for a JZX90 Chaser, verified by verbatim fetch rather than inferred.

**Three warnings from this pass that govern the merge stage.**

**Never map a Toyota colour name to a code across models.** Dark Green Mica is 6M1 on a 1995
MR2 and Celica but 6Q7 on a 1999 Altezza and Soarer. Red Mica is 3N1 on a Soarer and 3N2 on an
Aristo. The name is not the key; the model and year are.

**Codes were reused with different marketing names per market.** 576 is Super Bright Yellow in
Japan and Solar Yellow in the US; 3L2 is Super Red IV and Renaissance Red. **Merging on name alone
would split one colour into two.**

**Hex values from paint-matching sites are aftermarket estimates, not manufacturer data**, and at
least one is demonstrably wrong at source (it renders an orange as a red). Use them to rank shades
against each other, never as authoritative.

## Chaser Tourer V (JZX90)

Seven across the run, from Toyota's own grade pages across four sub-periods: Super White II (040),
Dark Turquoise Mica, Warm Grey Pearl Mica (046), Dark Bluish Grey Metallic (183, inferred), Silver
Metallic, Dark Green M.I.O., Dark Greyish Olive Metallic.

**Confidence: partial.** Names solid and grade-specific; codes almost entirely missing, because
every Japanese code database starts at the 1996 X100 generation and does not cover the X90.

**Gaps:** codes for the silver and both greens; what "M.I.O." denotes; any swatch for a
JZX90-exclusive colour.

## MR2 GT (SW20 Rev.3)

Seven on the GT grade: Super White II (040), Super Red II (3E5), Black (202), Super Bright Yellow
(576), **Strong Blue Metallic (8B6)**, Dark Green Mica (6M1), Bluish Grey Argentum Mica (187).

**Strong Blue Metallic is the iconic one**: a clean mid-bright metallic blue, introduced for Rev.3,
and tied to an Initial D character's car.

**Silver Metallic was NOT a GT colour.** It was exclusive to the Jan 1995 Bilstein Package, built
on the naturally aspirated G-Limited.

**Two code conflicts resolved against the databases**: Dark Green Mica is 6M1 here, not the 6Q7 the
final-code-sheet databases carry (that is the later Rev.5 code); and Bluish Grey Argentum is 187,
whose US listing reads "Bluish Green Arg." in what is almost certainly a transcription slip.

**Confidence: solid.**

## Celica GT-Four (ST205)

**The best-documented car found.** Six grade pages across the run, cross-checked against a survey
carrying per-colour production numbers.

Eleven across the run: Super White II (040), Super Red IV (3L2), Black (202), Silver Metallic
(199), Super Bright Yellow (576, only 148 built), Deep Teal Metallic (752), Greyish Turquoise Mica
Metallic (753), Dark Green Mica (6M1), Dark Bluish Grey Metallic (183), Blue Mica Metallic (8L5),
Light Green Metallic (6R0).

**Two findings worth carrying into the game:**

- **White was WRC-only at launch.** The Feb 1994 standard GT-Four had no white at all; only the
  homologation version could be had in Super White II. Given the Castrol livery, white plus the WRC
  wing is the ST205's signature look.
- **Light Green Metallic (6R0) was catalogued but never built.** Zero units. **It must not go in a
  pool as though a player could find one.**

**Confidence: solid.**

## Altezza RS200 Z Edition (SXE10)

Thirteen across six sub-periods of the Z Edition grade. The palette narrows sharply late: nine
colours in 2001, six by 2003 as the yellow and green are dropped.

**Confidence: partial.** Names and periods solid; two pearl-white codes unresolved, and the
JDM-only codes have no swatch source.

**Beige Metallic belongs to the luxury AS200 grades, not the Z Edition.**

## Chaser Tourer V (JZX100)

Eleven, all reachable on a Tourer V across its variants. Notable: **Prestigious Pearl Toning (2CF)
is a two-tone scheme**, and its component shades could not be established. It may not be
representable in a single-colour pool at all.

**Confidence: solid on names, codes and periods; thin on shades**, because most are JDM-only codes
with no swatch source.

## Soarer 2.5 GT-T (JZZ30)

**The most strongly corroborated car after the Celica**: two fully independent catalogue databases
give identical grade-specific lists across all three sub-periods, and Wikipedia confirms the running
change between them.

**Two things worth knowing for the game:**

- **The 2.5 GT-T was never available in a solid white.** Every white was a pearl and always a
  cost-extra manufacturer option. There is no Super White II on this car.
- **The four reds never overlap.** Super Red IV runs throughout, while Wine Red, Rose Red and Red
  Mica each occupy one period only, so **a JZZ30's red dates the car**.

The undated code databases are wrong by omission here, missing three colours from the run.

**Confidence: solid on names, codes and periods; thin on shades.**

## Mitsubishi, Subaru and European, six cars

A third surviving pass. It found two more contaminated sources and one methodological trap that
governs the whole merge.

**Cross-market name matching by paint code is unreliable and it bit twice.** Mitsubishi's R25 is
Kutani Red in Japan and Morocco Red Pearl in the US. **Use a code to confirm that a colour exists
and what finish it has, never to import a name or a description.**

**Two more aggregators are actively unsafe.** The main Italian paint database returns Jaguar and
Land Rover colours on Alfa 75 pages (Copperleaf Red, Arden Green, Bonatti Grey). General per-make
Japanese code lists carry only modern-era codes: the Subaru list contains none of the SVX's.

**Shade descriptions are the weakest part of the entire research effort, by a wide margin.** Text
sources give names, codes and finish type readily; almost none describe the actual hue. That is a
problem for consolidation, which can only merge from descriptions. The two exceptions worth noting
are the Mini, where an ICI cross-reference gives real descriptors, and one owner's account of the
SVX's Red Mica.

### VW Golf GTI 16V (Mk2)

**No source anywhere gives a German-market colour list for the GTI 16V trim.** What exists is
special-edition ties, Jetta 16V lists, and non-home markets, kept separate in the source material.

**Tornado Red (LY3D) appears on every 16V list in every market and every year checked**, which is
the strongest single finding. It was applied without a factory clear coat and is widely documented
fading toward pale pink-orange with age. **Dark Violet pearl (LC4V) is the Fire and Ice colour.**

**Confidence: partial.** Codes cross-verified across three or four sites; no primary brochure
readable; shades absent for all but Tornado Red and Capri Green.

### Subaru Alcyone SVX Version L

Six, unchanged across the run, with three independent Japanese sources in exact agreement: Black
Mica, White Mica, Crimson Mica, Red Mica, Dark Grey Metallic, and a **genuine factory two-tone in
silver and grey metallic that was not offered in the US**.

Crimson Mica, Red Mica and Bordeaux Mica are reportedly hard to tell apart in dull light, so they
sit in one dark-wine family rather than being distinct reds. **Red Mica is the rarest and least
popular SVX colour.**

**Confidence: solid** on names, weak on codes and shades. **The two-tone's panel arrangement is
not established**, which matters if the paint model cannot represent a two-tone at all.

### Mitsubishi Starion GSR-VR (A187A)

Five, all manufacturer-standard with no pearl or metallic markers: Sophia White (W09), Mexican Red,
Serbia Black (X15), Grace Silver (H84), Palermo Grey (L83).

**A window correction:** the archives record E-A187A as April 1988 to February 1990. The Feb 1987
GSR-VR was the 2.0 G63B car; the A187A is the 2.6 G54B.

**Confidence: solid.** Two independent Japanese archives agree on both grade records.

### Mini Cooper S Mk1

Eight, with a consistent and sourced pairing rule: **Tartan Red and Old English White bodies take a
black roof; every other body colour takes an Old English White roof.**

Almond Green is medium dark, not light (an earlier claim was the unreliable one). Old English White
is a cream, an off-white rather than a stark white. Surf Blue leans turquoise.

**Confidence: partial, close to solid.** A period BMC chart covering 1964-1967 contains all eight.
An Australian-built chart was found and deliberately excluded as a different market.

### Alfa Romeo 75 3.0 V6

Ten survive cross-checking, headed by **Rosso Alfa (555)**, the marque signature: a bright,
saturated, non-metallic red.

**Nothing found is specific to the 3.0 V6.** Italian Wikipedia's colour passages describe 1991
special editions on the 2.0 TS and 1.8 cars, not the V6, and that context was verified rather than
mis-attributed. Only one documented V6 car's colour surfaced: Grigio Canna di Fucile, a dark
gunmetal, on a 1989 America.

**Confidence: partial.**

### Mitsubishi GTO Twin Turbo (Z16A facelift)

Five: **Passion Red** (the standard, no-cost colour and the one the car is pictured in), Timber
Green pearl (G13), Pyrenees Black pearl (X08), Hamilton Silver metallic (A71), Galaxy White pearl
(W75).

**Mariana Blue is edge-of-window**: on the records to August 1997 and replaced by Timber Green, with
a possible brief overlap.

**Passion Red's code is unresolved between R38 and R71**, and this is the case that proves the
cross-market rule above: by period R71 fits, by name R38, and the same car demonstrably has a code
whose name differs between markets.

**Confidence: solid** on the colour set across six grade records, partial on codes.

## Classics and exotics, fourteen cars

Full tables in `factory-colours-deep-classics.md`. **6 solid, 7 partial, 1 thin.** What follows is
what that pass added to the method.

**`gazoo` fails on a special grade inside a longer-running model, and it failed twice.** It omitted
Titanium Grey Metallic from the RX-7 Spirit R Type A, and it listed the entire base NSX palette
against the NSX Type R. **On a limited or homologation grade, check the maker's own press release
first**; Mazda's and Honda's settled both cases. This is the single most useful correction to the
method so far, because the roster is full of exactly that kind of grade.

**Never-catalogued colours leak in from enthusiast photography, and must not be authored.** Three
were caught: the gold Toyota 2000GT (three show cars, a colour closer to ochre), the red Kenmeri
GT-R (seven custom orders, absent from the GT-R catalogue), and a claimed pair of Astral Silver
190 E Evo IIs that contradicts every exclusivity source. **A car existing in a colour is not the
same as that colour being a factory option**, and a pool built from photographs would be wrong in
a way nobody could later explain.

**Some cars had almost no choice at all, and that is a feature.** The **190 E 2.5-16 Evolution II
came in Blauschwarz Metallic 199 and nothing else**. The Kenmeri GT-R was about 130 white and 70
silver out of 197 built. A pool of one or two is a real answer, not a thin one, and it makes those
cars instantly recognisable when the wrong colour appears.

**Honda calls Championship White a 専用色, its own dedicated colour, and describes it as ivory
white rather than plain white.** Worth carrying into the palette: it is not the same white as
everyone else's.

**Remaining holes on this slice:** the Cosmo Sport 110S has no reachable factory palette at all and
needs a period Japanese catalogue scan; the 930 Turbo yielded one dated chart across eleven model
years, so 1978 to 1985 is unrecorded; and paint codes are absent for nearly every Japanese classic
here.

## JDM performance heroes, thirteen cars

Full tables in `factory-colours-deep-jdm.md`. **10 solid, 3 partial.** This slice carried the two
colours the whole feature was built around, and both turned out to be more specific than assumed.

### Bayside Blue is R34 only

**TV2, and it is not an R32 or an R33 colour.** The R34 range carried it from May 1998 and the GT-R
from January 1999. A deep saturated mid-blue metallic that reads steel-blue in sun and night-blue
in shade, warm rather than cold. **24.9 per cent of R34 GT-R production**, so it is common as well
as famous.

### Midnight Purple is three different colours, and none of them is R32

| code | car | built | shade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LP2** | BCNR33, from the **Jan 1995 launch**, standard on both GT-R and V-spec | - | deep purple pearl, pinkish-violet highlights, near-black in low light, **does not colour-shift** |
| **LV4** | BNR34, from Jan 1999 | 347 | shifts green to purple to bronze |
| **LX0** | BNR34 | 199 | lighter and bluer than LV4, gold-bronze flake |

**Several secondary sources wrongly call LP2 a late-R33 colour**; two dated catalogues put it at the
launch. **None of the three is GT-R exclusive**: LP2 also ran on the 180SX, S14 and Z32, LV4 on the
Silvia and Stagea, LX0 on the Stagea and 350Z.

**The R32's colour is Gun Grey Metallic (KH2)**, 37 per cent of 1992 production and the brochure
colour.

### Two more shade traps for consolidation

**Athlete Silver is filed under PURPLE** by the catalogue on two separate grade pages, so it is a
violet-cast silver rather than a neutral one. **Montego Blue Mica is filed under GREEN**, so it is
teal-leaning rather than a true blue. Both would be merged into the wrong bucket on their names
alone.

### One more contaminated source

**`32gt-r.com`** lists LP2, Active Red and "Aztec Red" for the R32 and contradicts the catalogue on
shared codes. Not to be mined.

### Gaps

**LX0's start month is genuinely unresolved**: Japanese sources say January 2000, English
aggregators 2001 to 2002, and the 133-of-199 V-spec split favours the earlier date. **Do not state
a window on this evidence.**

**The plain WRX STi Version VI page lists no blue at all** while the Type R page for the same months
does. That is either a real grade difference or a missed row, and **an Impreza with no blue in its
pool needs a human eye before it is authored.**

The 1992 BNR32 is not directly catalogued (the archive jumps August 1991 to February 1993), so the
1993 list stands as a proxy with the swap date unconfirmed.

**Next is consolidation**, and the traps above are its brief: merge on shade rather than name, never
map a name to a code across models or markets, and drop the colours that were catalogued but never
built or that exist only in enthusiast photographs.
