# Factory colours: the deep classics and exotics

Research notes for 14 cars whose colours are part of their identity. Roster order, keyed on
`rosterNo` in `midnight-garage-roster.csv`. Method inherited from
`factory-colours-research.md`: gazoo per-grade catalogue pages for the Japanese cars, factory
archives and marque registries for the European ones, and colour-family headings treated as shade
evidence.

**Nothing here is authored into content yet.** Where a shade could not be sourced it says so; no
colour in this file is invented.

**How to read the shade column.** It describes the paint as it actually looks, because
consolidation into shared pools works from appearance, not from marketing names. Where a shade
description is inferred from the colour's name plus period photography rather than from a written
source, the row says so.

## 68. BMW M3 (E30), 1988-1991

## 70. Nissan Fairlady 240ZG (HS30-H), 1972-1973

**A three-colour car.** Every source consulted agrees the ZG was sold in the three "Grand Prix"
colours and nothing else, which makes this a short list by fact rather than by thin research.

| name | Japanese | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grand Prix White | グランプリホワイト | plain solid white, no metallic | yes, the racing-homologation look |
| Grand Prix Red | グランプリレッド | bright solid red | yes |
| Grand Prix Maroon | グランプリマルーン | deep dark red-brown, close to burgundy, near-black in shade | **yes, the ZG colour** |

Grand Prix Maroon was mixed specifically for the 240ZG at the direction of Nissan's colour designer
of the day, and was the ZG's exclusive colour (offered as an option on other S30 grades). Nissan's
own heritage material singles it out.

**Confidence: partial**, and the reason is a dating conflict worth recording rather than papering
over. `supercarnostalgia` presents all three as available to ZG buyers. `nosweb`'s survey of every
first-generation Z colour puts **Grand Prix Maroon and Grand Prix Red at the April 1973 change**,
which would mean a 1971-72 ZG was Grand Prix White only. A follow-up search returned sources
asserting each way. **For a roster car dated 1972-1973 the pool spans the change either way, so
all three are defensible; a 1971-only car would not be.**

**Gap:** no paint codes found for any of the three. No written shade description for Maroon beyond
its name and photographs.

Sources: <https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/nissan-fairlady-240zg> ,
<https://nosweb.jp/nostalgichero/articles/detail/2242> ,
<https://n-link.nissan.co.jp/NOM/ARCHIVE/03/>

## 79. Mazda RX-7 Spirit R Type A (FD3S), 2002

**Five colours, from Mazda's own press release of 25 March 2002.** This is a factory source, so it
settles the entry.

| name | Japanese | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Titanium Grey Metallic | チタニウムグレーメタリック | mid metallic grey with a faint warm bronze cast, deliberately un-flashy | **yes, the Spirit R colour** |
| Pure White | ピュアホワイト | plain solid white, no pearl; the maker-standard colour | yes |
| Vintage Red | ヴィンテージレッド | deep dark red, noticeably darker and richer than a bright primary red | yes |
| Innocent Blue Mica | イノセントブルーマイカ | mid blue mica, clean and slightly bright rather than navy | yes |
| Brilliant Black | ブリリアントブラック | solid black with clear coat, no pearl or flake | no |

**Titanium Grey Metallic was a Spirit R series exclusive** (Mazda's release marks it
"スピリットRシリーズ専用色"), it is what the last RX-7 off the Ujina line wore, and it is the
colour the car is remembered in.

**A source conflict, resolved.** The gazoo per-grade catalogue page for the Spirit R Type A lists
only four colours (Pure White maker-standard, with Vintage Red, Innocent Blue Mica and Brilliant
Black as maker options) and omits Titanium Grey entirely. **Mazda's press release wins**: it names
five and marks the grey as the series-exclusive. This is the first case found where a gazoo grade
page is demonstrably short, and it is worth remembering for the rest of the roster.

**Confidence: solid.** Factory press release plus a per-grade catalogue page.

**Gap:** no paint codes found. Standard-versus-option status comes from gazoo, which is the list
missing a colour, so treat that split as indicative.

Sources: <https://newsroom.mazda.com/ja/publicity/release/2002/200203/0325.html> ,
<https://gazoo.com/catalog/maker/MAZDA/RX7/199710/2502954/>

## 80. Nissan Fairlady Z432 (PS30), 1969-1973

The Z432 did not have its own palette: it drew on the first-generation Z range's colours, which at
the November 1969 launch numbered seven.

| name | Japanese | standard or option | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silver Grey | シルバーグレー | standard | light neutral grey, low lustre | no |
| Monte Carlo Red | モンテカルロレッド | standard | bright solid red | yes |
| Sunshine Yellow | サンシャインイエロー | standard | warm bright yellow, slightly golden rather than acid | no |
| Safari Gold | サファリゴールド | standard | warm ochre-gold, a tan-leaning metallic | yes, the period Z colour |
| Kilimanjaro White | キリマンジャロホワイト | option | plain solid white | no |
| Racing Green | レーシンググリーン | option | deep dark green | no |
| New Sight Orange | ニューサイトオレンジ | option | vivid saturated orange | **yes** |

**The Z432-R wore New Sight Orange as standard with a dark gun-grey bonnet**, which is the single
most recognisable first-generation Z image there is. One source calls the 432-R's colours
"Grand Prix Silver" and "Grand Prix Orange" rather than the launch names; that naming is not
corroborated and is recorded here as unresolved.

**Confidence: partial.** The seven launch names are solid and come from a survey article dedicated
to first-generation Z colours. What is not established is which of the seven remained on the
options list across the Z432's whole 1969-1973 life, and the S30 palette is known to have changed
at least twice in that window (metallics, Light Blue Metallic, Wine, Dark Purple and others arrive
from 1973).

**Gaps:** no paint codes for any Z432 colour; no written shade descriptions, so the shade column is
inferred from names and period photography; the 432-R colour-naming conflict above.

Sources: <https://nosweb.jp/nostalgichero/articles/detail/2242> ,
<https://meisha.co.jp/?p=1748>

## 82. Porsche 911 Turbo 3.3 (930), 1978-1989

**The hardest car in this set to pin down**, because the roster window is eleven model years and
Porsche changed the chart annually, offered a large special-order metallic range on top, and sold
Paint to Sample throughout. No single accessible source gives a clean year-by-year 930 chart:
paintref returns 403, the uniquecarsandparts 1986 chart is a dead domain, stuttcars defers to a
database it does not inline, and Rennlist blocks fetching.

### The 1986 chart, which is the one dated list that could be sourced

| name | code | standard or special order | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grand Prix White | 908 | standard | plain solid white, no metallic; the racing white | **yes** |
| Guards Red | 027 | standard | bright saturated solid red, slightly orange-leaning | **yes, the 1980s 911 colour** |
| Black | 700 | standard | plain solid black | **yes** |
| Carmine Red | 80F | standard | deep solid red with a blue cast, darker than Guards | no |
| Dark Blue | 347 | standard | very dark solid navy | no |
| Caramel Beige | 499 | standard | warm mid beige, tan-leaning | no |
| Turquoise | 21M | standard | light solid blue-green | no |
| Summer Yellow | 10W | standard | bright warm solid yellow | no |
| Silver Metallic | 980 | special order | classic bright metallic silver | yes |
| Diamond Blue Metallic | 697 | special order | pale ice-blue metallic | no |
| Venetian Blue Metallic | 35U | special order | mid blue metallic | no |
| Marine Blue Metallic | 35V | special order | dark blue metallic | no |
| Lagoon Green Metallic | 35Y | special order | mid blue-green metallic | no |
| Granite Green Metallic | 699 | special order | dark grey-green metallic | no |
| Nougat Brown Metallic | 40B | special order | mid warm brown metallic | no |
| Espresso Brown Metallic | 40D | special order | very dark brown metallic | no |
| Cassis Red Metallic | 80D | special order | dark blackcurrant red metallic | no |

**The three that carry the car's identity are Guards Red, Grand Prix White and Black**, all three
available across essentially the whole 930 run, and between them they account for the overwhelming
majority of period 911 Turbos. A 930 pool that offers those three plus Silver Metallic is
defensible for any year in the window.

**A naming trap.** US-market documentation renames some of these: what the German chart calls
Indischrot appears as Guards Red, and one US-facing code list shows "Alpine White (90E)" and
"India Red" where the factory chart shows Grand Prix White (908) and Guards Red. **Use the
German-chart names for a period Porsche**; the codes are the reliable identity.

**Confidence: partial.** The 1986 chart is corroborated across two reads of the same Rennlist
thread and its codes match Porsche's known numbering, but it is one forum-sourced year.

**Gaps:** no sourced chart for 1978-1985 or 1987-1989, so early-930 exclusives (the period brown,
gold and green metallics of the late 1970s) are unrecorded here. No shade descriptions from a
written source; the shade column is inferred from names, codes and period photography. Paint to
Sample means any individual 930 may legitimately wear a colour on no chart at all.

Sources: <https://rennlist.com/forums/911-turbo-930-forum/738848-930-turbo-1986-colors.html>
(fetch blocked, read through search) , <https://www.bigporsche.com/paint911.htm> ,
<https://www.porscheknowledge.com/colour-codes-by-year/>

## 84. Nissan Skyline 2000GT-R Hakosuka (KPGC10), 1970-1972

**A three-colour car that becomes a two-colour car**, which is exactly the kind of shape the pools
need to respect.

| name | Japanese | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White | ホワイト | plain solid white | **yes, the definitive GT-R image** |
| Silver Metallic | シルバーメタリック | bright metallic silver | **yes** |
| Skyline Brown | スカイラインブラウン | soft warm brown with a creamy sheen, gentler and less yellow than Nissan's Safari Brown | no, but it is the collector's rarity |

**Skyline Brown was set only on the early hardtop GT-R and was gone by 1972**, leaving White and
Silver Metallic. It sold so poorly that surviving original-paint examples are treated as near
mythical, and the nosweb feature that documents it exists precisely because the colour is so rarely
seen. Its shade description above is the one genuinely sourced shade in this entry: nosweb
describes it as giving "a cream-like sheen, a gentle impression", explicitly distinguishing it from
the yellower Safari Brown used elsewhere in the Nissan range.

**Confidence: partial.** One specialist feature article plus corroborating secondary summaries.
Names are consistent across sources; nothing gave a factory catalogue scan.

**Gaps:** no paint codes; no exact date for the deletion of Skyline Brown beyond "by 1972"; the
racing-team white-with-red-stripe and works liveries are not factory colours and are excluded.

Sources: <https://nosweb.jp/nostalgichero/articles/detail/4694> ,
<http://www.nostalgic.co.jp/car_archive/car/kpgc10.html>

## 85. Mazda Cosmo Sport 110S (Series II, L10B), 1972

**The weakest entry in this file, and the honest answer is that the list could not be closed.**
Seven searches across Japanese and English sources produced no factory colour chart for the Cosmo
Sport. The Japanese Mazda body-colour database that would have answered it is encoding-corrupted;
minkara's L10B spec page has both body-colour fields empty; Japanese Wikipedia names no colour for
the first-generation car; Mazda's own history page does not list a palette.

| name | shade | status of the evidence | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swan White | plain solid white | **the only name found attached to the model in a source**, and repeatedly given as the car's original and most common colour | **yes, the Cosmo is a white car** |
| Red | solid red, shade not established | reported as available; no catalogue name found | no |
| Silver | metallic silver, shade not established | reported as available; no catalogue name found | no |

Secondary and auction sources additionally mention grey, yellow, orange, cream and a metallic wine
red on individual cars. **None of those is corroborated by a catalogue source and none should be
authored from this file.** Two known light blue and light-green prototypes with contrasting roofs
were pre-production cars, not catalogue colours.

**Confidence: thin.** One colour name is well attested; the rest of the palette is not established.

**Gap, stated plainly:** the Cosmo Sport needs a period Japanese catalogue scan or a marque
registry. A 1968 dealer brochure is offered on eBay and would settle it. Until then the safe pool
is Swan White plus, at most, a plain red and a silver.

## 86. Mercedes-Benz 190 E 2.5-16 Evolution II, 1990

**A one-colour car.** All 502 road cars left the factory in the same paint, and that is the whole
identity of the model's look.

| name | code | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blauschwarz Metallic (Blue-Black Metallic) | 199 | near-black with a deep blue metallic cast, reads black in shade and blue-black in direct sun | **yes, and it is the only one** |

RM Sotheby's calls it "the model's exclusive colour combination of Blue-Black Metallic over black
leather", and marque forums state that both the Evolution and the Evolution II were delivered in
code 199 only.

**One unresolved claim.** A secondary source states that two of the 502 cars were finished in
Astral Silver. It is not corroborated, and it contradicts the "exclusive colour" framing used by
the auction houses and the marque community. **Do not author a silver Evo II.** If the claim is
ever confirmed it describes two cars in the world, which is not a colour pool.

**Confidence: solid** on the single colour and its code; the two-silver-cars claim is explicitly
flagged as unconfirmed.

**Gap:** Mercedes' own public archive page returns 403, so this rests on auction and community
sources rather than the factory archive.

## 87. Nissan Skyline 2000GT-R Kenmeri (KPGC110), 1973

**197 cars, and the colour split is known car by car.** This is the best-documented entry in the
file after the Evo II.

| name | Japanese | cars built | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White | ホワイト | about 130 | plain solid white | **yes** |
| Silver | シルバー | about 70 | metallic silver; the only colour Nissan photographed the GT-R in | **yes, the catalogue colour** |
| Red | 赤 | 7 | solid red, exact shade not established | no, but it is the rarity |

**Red was not a GT-R catalogue colour.** The GT-R's own catalogue listed no colour options at all
and showed only silver cars. The Kenmeri range catalogue showed its image colours as blue metallic,
white, silver and deep green; red appeared only in the earlier Hardtop 1600/1800 catalogue, marked
"body colour is a custom order colour". The seven red GT-Rs were therefore special commissions.

**A pool-authoring note.** If the game wants a Kenmeri GT-R that reads right, it is white or
silver, roughly two to one. Red is a 3.5 per cent car and should be treated as a rarity if it
appears at all. The blue metallic and deep green of the Kenmeri range catalogue belong to the
ordinary Kenmeri, **not** to the GT-R.

**Confidence: solid.** A dedicated feature article on the red cars that cites the period catalogues
directly, with per-colour build numbers.

**Gaps:** no paint codes; no established shade for the red; the exact catalogue names behind
"White" and "Silver" were not found.

Sources: <https://nosweb.jp/nostalgichero/articles/detail/8034> ,
<https://www.automesseweb.jp/2018/04/28/53361>

## 88. Honda NSX-R (NA1), 1992-1995

**A one-colour car**, on Honda's own wording, twice.

| name | Japanese | code | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Championship White | チャンピオンシップホワイト | NH-0 (Honda's standard Championship White code) | ivory-cast solid white, warmer and creamier than a pure white, no pearl | **yes, and it is the point of the car** |

Honda's November 1992 launch release calls it "1965年のホンダＦ－１初優勝マシンを彷彿させる
アイボリーホワイトの専用色", an ivory-white dedicated colour evoking the RA272 that took Honda's
first Formula One win. Honda's own NSX 30th anniversary retrospective repeats that it is the
NSX-R's 専用カラー. **The shade description is sourced, not inferred: Honda itself says ivory
white, not plain white.**

**A gazoo failure, the second in this set.** The gazoo per-grade page for NSX Type R
(1992.11-1994.02) lists the entire base NSX palette against the grade: Grand Prix White, Formula
Red, Charlotte Green Pearl, Berlina Black, Sebring Silver Metallic as standard, with Neutron White
Pearl / Championship White, Indy Yellow Pearl and Kaiser Silver Metallic as options.
**That is the NSX's list, not the NSX-R's**, and it contradicts Honda's own 専用色 wording. Taken
with the RX-7 Spirit R case, the rule to carry forward is: **on a limited or homologation grade,
check the maker's press release before trusting a gazoo grade page.**

**Confidence: solid** on Championship White as the NSX-R's exclusive colour, from two Honda
sources.

**Gap:** no source explicitly stated "no other colour was ever delivered", and the NH-0 code is
Honda's general Championship White code carried over from the Integra Type R research rather than
one found on an NSX-R document.

Sources: <https://global.honda/jp/news/1992/4921126.html> ,
<https://www.honda.co.jp/sportscar/NSX_30th/type-r/> ,
<https://gazoo.com/catalog/maker/HONDA/NSX/199009/2001771/> (the contradicting list)

source.

## 93. Toyota 2000GT (MF10), 1969-1970

**Three colours became six**, and the roster's car is on the later side of that change, so all six
apply.

| name | Japanese | 前期 (1967-68) | 後期 (1969-70) | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pegasus White | ペガサスホワイト | standard | standard | plain solid white; the original formula no longer exists and restorers mix their own | **yes** |
| Solar Red | ソーラーレッド | standard | standard | bright solid red | **yes, the Bond-era hero colour** |
| Thunder Silver Metallic | サンダーシルバーメタリック | standard | standard | metallic silver | yes |
| Bellatrix Yellow | ベラトリクスイエロー | special order only | standard | bright solid yellow | no |
| Atlantis Green | アトランティスグリーン | special order only | standard | deep green | no |
| Twilight Turquoise Metallic | トワイライトターコイズメタリック | special order only | standard | metallic blue-green, a dark turquoise; also recorded simply as a blue metallic | no |

**The three later colours existed before they were catalogued**: they were built in tiny numbers as
special orders on the early car and were promoted to the catalogue for the late type. **Gold was
never a catalogue colour.** Exactly three gold 2000GTs were painted, two early cars for the 1967
Tokyo Motor Show and one late car (chassis 10232) in "Aflare Gold" for the 1969 show, and Japanese
sources describe the finish as closer to ochre than to a modern metallic gold. **Do not author a
gold 2000GT.**

**Confidence: solid** on the six names and the early/late split; the Toyota Automobile Museum
independently confirms the early three.

**Gaps:** no paint codes; no written shade descriptions beyond the names, so the shade column is
inferred; the exact date within 1969 at which the extra three were catalogued was not pinned down.

## 94. Lexus LFA, 2010

**Ten standard colours and a special-order range of about twenty more.** The LFA was effectively
bespoke: Lexus quotes thirty exterior colours in total, of which ten were the catalogue set.

### The ten standard colours

| name (US market) | code | shade | iconic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whitest White | 079 | the purest, coldest solid white Lexus offered; the LFA's launch and press colour | **yes** |
| Pearl White | 077 | warm pearl white with a soft glow | no |
| Metallic Silver | 1F2 | bright clean metallic silver | no |
| Pearl Grey | 1G0 | mid grey with a pearl lustre | no |
| Black | 212 | plain solid black | no |
| Pearl Yellow | 5B0 | strong warm yellow with a pearl depth; the colour Lexus chose to reveal in August 2010 | **yes** |
| Pearl Brown | 4V2 | dark warm brown pearl, closer to bronze than to chocolate | yes, the connoisseur's LFA |

### The special-order range

Steel Grey (9H9), Starlight Black (9J2), Matte Black (9K4), Ivory White (9J8), Crystal Gold (9H7),
Orange (9K5), Sunset Orange (9K6), Mint Green (9K1), Lime Green (9J6), Fresh Green (9J7), Sky Blue
(9J5), Slate Blue (9K3), Steel Blue (9J3), Black Amethyst (9J0), Lapis Lazuli (9H8), Brownstone
(9J1), and a Pearl Grey over Steel Grey two-tone (L383). Three further codes were allocated and
never used: Passionate Pink (9J4), Lavender (9J9), Moss Green (9K2). **Orange and Matte Black are
the two special-order colours with real recognition**, both through the Nurburgring Package, which
was restricted to four colours: Red, Matte Black, Orange and Whitest White.

**The naming caveat, and it is the important part of this entry.** Every name above except Whitest
White comes from a US-market archive. **A code proves a colour exists and what finish it has; it
does not carry a name across markets.** The only JDM name confirmed in a Japanese source is
ホワイテストホワイト (Whitest White). The remaining nine standard names should be treated as
US-market labels until a Japanese catalogue is found, even though Lexus Japan's naming for this car
appears to be katakana transliterations of the same English words.

**Confidence: partial.** The code list is solid and complete; the JDM names are not confirmed. The
sources that would have settled it all failed: gazoo's LFA grade page does not render its body
colour section, japanesecartrade and the MOTA catalogue both return 403, and the carsensor page
offers only a used-car "white or black" search filter, which is the goo-net trap the method doc
warns about.

**Gap:** a Japanese-market LFA colour list with katakana names.

## What this pass adds to the method

Four things worth carrying into the remaining roster work.

**1. On a limited or homologation grade, the maker's press release beats the catalogue archive.**
gazoo was wrong twice here and in the same way: it showed four colours for the RX-7 Spirit R Type A
and omitted the series-exclusive Titanium Grey, and it showed the entire base NSX palette against
the NSX Type R grade when Honda's own release calls Championship White the car's 専用色. Both
failures are on cars where a special grade sits inside a longer-running model. gazoo remains the
right first stop for an ordinary grade.

**2. Short lists are the normal answer for this class of car.** Of the fourteen, one is a
single-colour car (190 E Evo II), two are three-colour cars (240ZG, Hakosuka GT-R), one is
effectively single-colour (NSX-R), and the Kenmeri GT-R is two colours plus seven special
commissions. Where a list came back short, it came back short because the car was short, not
because the search stopped early.

**3. European classics were not uniformly easier than the JDM cars.** The Evo II, the Countach and
the F355 were straightforward. The 930 was the hardest car in the set, because eleven model years
of annually-changing charts plus Paint to Sample defeats every free source: paintref 403s, Rennlist
403s, uniquecarsandparts is a parked domain, stuttcars defers to a database it does not inline.

**4. The gold and near-gold traps.** Two cars here have a famous colour that was never a catalogue
colour: the gold 2000GT (three cars, show cars) and the red Kenmeri GT-R (seven cars, custom
order). Both are exactly the sort of thing that leaks into a colour pool from enthusiast
photography.
