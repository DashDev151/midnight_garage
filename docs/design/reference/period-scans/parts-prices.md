# Period Japanese aftermarket parts prices, 1994 to 2004

Everything below was read off a period source and transcribed. Nothing is
estimated. Where a digit was uncertain it is flagged in place; there are only a
couple of those.

## Sources, and how to read them

| Tag | Source | Date | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `HKS94` | HKS `SUPER TECHNOLOGY HIGH-LIGHTS 1994` printed catalogue, photographed. Saved as `1994-00_hks-super-technology-highlights_*-TUN_0*.jpg` | 1994 (printed on the cover) | Bare yen figures beside the product photograph. Part only, no fitting. Tax basis not printed on the page. |
| `HKS99` | Internet Archive captures of `hks-power.co.jp/goods_master/`, HKS's own online `GOODS MASTER` catalogue | captures 1999-09 to 2000-05, per row | Column headed `価格` or `Price（小売価格）`, retail. Part only. Tax basis not stated. |
| `CUS02` | Internet Archive capture of `cusco.co.jp/03lst_lsd/`, snapshot `20021216` | 2002 | Bare yen figures in a fitment table. Part only. |
| `END04` | Internet Archive capture of `endless-sport.co.jp/product-endless/brake-caliper/`, snapshot `20041112` | 2004 | **States both**: tax-inclusive first, `本体価格` in brackets. |
| `RAY99` | Internet Archive capture of `rayswheels.co.jp/1.html`, snapshot `19990210` | 1999-02 | Bare yen figures, **per wheel**. Part only. |

**On tax.** Only `END04` prints the basis, and it prints both figures, which is
how we know its bracketed number is 本体価格 and its headline number includes
the 5 per cent consumption tax of the day (238,000 x 1.05 = 249,900, and it
checks out on every row). The others print a bare yen figure with no note
anywhere on the page. Japanese catalogue practice through this whole period was
to quote 本体価格, that is the part on its own excluding both consumption tax
and fitting, so **treat every figure in this document as 本体価格 unless the row
says otherwise**. Consumption tax was 3 per cent to March 1997 and 5 per cent
from April 1997.

**On fitting.** No catalogue price here includes fitting. Every one is the part
in a box. Only one period labour figure turned up at all and it is in the brakes
table: Endless charged `¥10,500` including tax, `¥10,000` 本体, to overhaul one
caliper, with consumables and carriage extra. That is the only period shop rate
this sweep produced.

**On brand level.** Everything below is a premium tuning brand. HKS, Endless,
Cusco and RAYS were the top of the market, and Volk TE37 is a forged wheel.
**No budget or shop-brand prices were found**, so the bottom rung of every
ladder here is "the cheapest thing a famous maker sold", not "the cheapest thing
you could buy". Treat these as the upper band and assume a real budget tier sat
well below them.

## The shape of the ladder

What the numbers say, category by category. Where a category has a genuine
three-rung shape in the sources it is given as cheap / middle / top; where the
sources only reach part of the range that is said plainly.

**Exhaust.** The best-populated category, and the ladder is clear. A front pipe
or a tail section is `¥21,500` to `¥38,000`. A complete muffler runs `¥42,000`
at the bottom of the Legal range, `¥62,800` to `¥78,000` for the volume sellers,
`¥88,000` to `¥98,000` for the Super Dragger and Dual Dragger, and tops out at
`¥128,000` to `¥136,000` for the flagship. Add `¥65,000` if the car needs a
metal catalyser too. **Roughly: cheap ¥45k, middle ¥80k, top ¥130k, a spread of
about 3x**, and about 6x if you count a front pipe alone as the entry move. Note
how tight this is compared with everything else: exhaust was the affordable
modification, which is exactly why every catalogue led with it.

**Suspension.** Four distinct rungs and a very wide spread. Springs alone are
`¥18,000` a pair for a universal racing spring, or `¥37,000` to `¥40,000` for a
car-specific set. A damper kit without the springs is `¥127,000` to `¥148,000`.
A full coilover kit is `¥178,000` to `¥208,000`. A 4-way adjustable racing
damper is `¥538,000`. **Cheap ¥20k to ¥40k, middle ¥150k, top ¥210k, with a
race tier at ¥540k**, so about 12x from springs to full coilovers and 30x to the
race item. Add `¥19,800` per pillowball upper mount on top.

**Brakes.** Also four rungs, and the widest spread of any category. Pads alone
are `¥25,000` 本体. A caliper set with pads is `¥168,000` to `¥198,000`, but it
is useless without a car-specific stay at `¥35,000`. A full system kit is
`¥288,000` to `¥408,000` depending on rotor size and construction. **Cheap ¥25k,
middle ¥200k, top ¥400k, about 16x.** Worth noting for the game: pads are the
one genuinely cheap braking upgrade and everything above them jumps by a factor
of eight in one step.

**Intake and turbo.** The widest range in the whole document. A filter element
is `¥3,900`. An induction kit is `¥37,800` to `¥39,800`. A blow-off valve is
`¥49,800`. Plumbing bits like an extension kit are `¥25,000` to `¥48,000`. Then
it steps hard: a bolt-on turbine kit for an ordinary turbo car is `¥182,000` to
`¥202,000`, the same for a twin-turbo GT-R is `¥320,000` to `¥358,000`, and a
single-turbo conversion is `¥438,000` to `¥548,000`. An intercooler kit was
`¥248,000` in 1994. **Cheap ¥4k, middle ¥40k, top ¥550k.** No other category has
anything like this range, and the ladder is really two ladders: bolt-on intake
parts under `¥50,000`, and turbine work starting at `¥182,000` with nothing in
between.

**Engine management.** Three clean rungs. Single-function boxes (turbo timer,
fuel-cut defencer, air-fuel meter, ignition amplifier) cluster at `¥11,800` to
`¥12,800`. A boost controller is `¥42,800` for the cut-down EVC EZ or `¥64,800`
for the full EVC. A piggyback or standalone ECU is `¥88,800` to `¥100,000`, and
always needs a car-specific harness at `¥10,000` to `¥20,000` on top, which is

**Drivetrain.** Only LSDs, and the ladder is unusually flat: `¥75,000` for the
cheaper grade, `¥88,000` for the dearer, `¥104,000` for the supercharged
applications. **A spread of only about 1.4x.** Clutches and flywheels were not
reached.

**Wheels.** One data point on the market, but a thorough one. A forged Volk
TE37 was `¥30,000` a wheel in 13 inch and `¥69,000` a wheel in 18 inch, so a set
of four ran `¥120,000` to `¥276,000`, and size drives price far more than
anything else does. This is the top of the wheel market; a cast wheel would have
been a fraction of it and no period figure for one was found. Fitting extras are
small and worth modelling as a nuisance cost: caps `¥2,000` to `¥2,600`, nuts
`¥550` to `¥600` each, hub rings `¥500`.

**Interior.** Thin. A mechanical gauge was `¥11,500` to `¥12,500`, and a console
to mount three of them `¥26,000`. Seats, harnesses, wheels and cages were not
reached.

**Aero.** No data.

**Two cross-cutting observations.** First, **the accessories are never trivial**:
a harness at `¥20,000` on a `¥100,000` ECU, a stay at `¥35,000` on a `¥168,000`
caliper set, and a metal catalyser at `¥65,000` to make an exhaust legal. In
period these were a real and irritating fraction of the bill, typically 20 to 35
per cent, and a game that prices only the headline part will feel too cheap.
Second, **labour is almost invisible in this material**. Catalogues quote the
part and stop. The single shop rate found anywhere was Endless charging
`¥10,000` 本体 to overhaul one caliper. If we want a period anchor for fitting
rates, catalogues are the wrong source and the small shop adverts at the back of
`Option` and `CARBOY` are the right one, which is the gap the blocked auction
route left open.
