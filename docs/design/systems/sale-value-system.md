## 0. What v4 changed

absolute day clock double-charged the specialist, because liquidity already makes them wait and
the quality slide then taxed the waiting. A build that suited one buyer would have arrived at a

**The fix is one clock, normalised by how often the car is actually seen.** Staleness and offer

not go stale on the shelf; it goes stale when people have looked and passed.

Measured, not asserted: Path 1's expected accepted quality is **0.974**, not the 0.897 an

## 1. The problem

1. **Modifying a car to sell it loses money, always.** Parts retain a flat 55 per cent.
2. **Taste is flat.** A mean of five anti-correlated stats sits near the middle by construction.
3. **Nothing about a build changes how hard a car is to sell**, and reputation reads only
   condition and authenticity, so a grenade sells as `clean`.
4. **Time is free.** Flat costs are noise, so the correct play is to skip days.

## 2. The principle, and the tripartite law

**A sale has three outcomes: what you get, how long you wait, and what it does to your name.**

No stage may price the same QUESTION twice. Coherence is read five times, deliberately:

| stage | what it asks of coherence |
| --- | --- |
| C, the discount | What does the market expect this build to cost in failures? |
| D, retention | Is this collection of parts worth what was paid for it? |
| E, the match | How much does *this particular buyer* mind? |
| F, liquidity | How many buyers mind enough to walk away? |
| G, standing | Does selling this reflect well on the shop? |

**The tripartite law, true today only by accident and now written down:**

- **Condition enters at Stage B and nowhere else.** A yen of parts clears a yen of bill.
- **Coherence enters at C through G**, per the table above.
- **Build intensity enters at Stage E only**, through the reliability stat that feeds the match.

## 3. The price stack

### Stage A

    cleanValue = bookValueYen * mileageFactor(mileageKm) * (heatPercent / 100)

### Stage B

    conditionValue = cleanValue
                   - marketRepairDiscount * billBelow
                   - beyondDiscount[tier] * billAbove
    conditionValue = max(conditionValue, scrapValueFraction * cleanValue)

`marketRepairDiscount` 1.3. `beyondDiscount`: 0.4 entry, 0.8 everyday, 1.2 enthusiast,
1.3 flagship.

**A yen of parts clears exactly a yen of bill**, never more. The only mechanism permitted to
break that identity is salvage harvesting, and it does so by changing what parts **cost**,
never what they **clear**.

**Tier expectation bands must be published with these levers**, because `billAbove` is
`billToMint - billToExpected`. A flagship expecting `mint` has `billAbove` identically zero, so
its `beyondDiscount` of 1.3 multiplies nothing and is dead until machining creates an
above-mint state. **The restoration business therefore lives at enthusiast tier**, where the
expected band is `fine` and 1.2 is real. Path 4 works it.

### Stage C

    coherenceDiscount = coherenceDiscountWeight * (1 - coherenceFactor) * tolerance[buyer]
    stagedValue       = conditionValue * (1 - coherenceDiscount)

`coherenceDiscountWeight` **0.35**. `tolerance` defaults to **1.0**; **stancer 0.0**,
**tuner 0.5**. **Taste-blind exits price at the default tolerance.**

### Stage D

    retention       = retentionFloor + (retentionCeiling - retentionFloor) * coherenceFactor
    creditedPremium = partsValue * retention * aftermarketReturn[tier] * foundationFactor

`retentionFloor` **0.30**, `retentionCeiling` **1.10**. `retention` is not reduced by
tolerance: a bodged install is a bodged install to everyone.

### Stage E

    shortfall(stat) = max(0, target[stat] - s) + max(0, s - upper[stat])
    match           = clamp(1 - sum(importance * shortfall) / sum(importance), 0, 1)

**Exceeding a target earns nothing: the buyer is satisfied, not impressed.** The upper bound is
what makes "modification narrows your market" a mechanism rather than an authoring convention.

    standard channel (ceiling <= 1 + tasteSpread):
        multiplier = min(1 - tasteSpread + 2 * tasteSpread * match, ceiling)
    premium channel (ceiling > 1 + tasteSpread):
        multiplier = (1 - tasteSpread) + (ceiling - (1 - tasteSpread)) * match

## 4. Stage F: liquidity, on one normalised clock

    offersSeen  = expected offers this listing has already produced
    staleness   = stalenessFloor + (1 - stalenessFloor) * exp(-offersSeen / stalenessHalfLifeOffers)
    qualityMean = qualityFresh - (qualityFresh - qualityFloor)
                                 * (1 - exp(-offersSeen / qualityHalfLifeOffers))

    dailyOfferChance = channelOfferFactor * staleness
                     * sum over b of presence(b) * weight(b)
    offerYen = channelPrice * clamp(Normal(qualityMean, qualitySpread), qualityFloor, 1.0)

**Both curves read `offersSeen`, never `daysListed`.** That is the whole fix. A specialist
build is already rare-to-sell through `presence`; charging it again on an absolute calendar
would tax the same scarcity twice and sink every listed path below the taste-blind exits.

Proposed: `stalenessFloor` **0.35**, `stalenessHalfLifeOffers` **3.5**, `qualityFresh`
**0.98**, `qualityFloor` **0.86**, `qualityHalfLifeOffers` **3.0**, `qualitySpread` **0.04**.

This delivers, in one mechanism: the price decay §4 previously only promised; the lowball, as
the day-3 offer at 0.96 you refuse and the day-19 at 0.88 you stare at; and the texture of a
listing week.

**Offers wear their archetype's face.** A disappointing number is often the *wrong buyer*
rather than a lowball, and the player can see which. Most of the week's character, free.

**Relisting returns to `relistRecovery` = 0.70 of fresh, never to 1.0.** Same plate, same
advertisement, everyone has seen it. A full reset would sell patience back for a fee.

## 5. Stage G: standing

### The lemon predicate, in full

v3 dissolved the threshold into a probability and lost the deterministic legs with it, leaving
Path 3's risk with no source. Both halves are needed, and the split is the game's own
looks-versus-truth theme rendered into the reputation rules:

- **Deterministic, for what a buyer can SEE.** Any scrap or missing part, or a condition
  average below its threshold, is a lemon. No roll. Path 3's rough car qualifies here.
- **Probabilistic, for what is HIDDEN.** Coherence is not visible on a forecourt, so it is
  whether the buyer catches it:

      lemonRisk = clamp((lemonRiskCeiling - coherenceFactor)
                        / (lemonRiskCeiling - lemonRiskFloor), 0, 1)

  `lemonRiskCeiling` **0.65**, `lemonRiskFloor` **0.45**. Path 2's stupid car at coherence
  0.605 carries **22.5 per cent**, shown in the appraisal as exactly that.

### Concours means original

**Concours** requires mint condition, high coherence **and high authenticity**. A mint,
coherent, **modified** car earns a **magazine feature** instead, which is the period-correct
honour and carries the **provenance multiplier**. That single split closes concours semantics,
the provenance placeholder, and the "should `retentionCeiling` exceed 1.10" question. It should
not.

**The feature is an offered event, not a threshold crossed silently.** A qualifying magazine
listing draws an offer to shoot the car: hold it a week, and it gains the provenance multiplier
plus a small heat blip on its own model. **Players can pump their own market, and that is
allowed** — it is a real thing a famous shop could do.

### Who saw it? The cars resurface

A cash sale to a complicit fixer damaging your public name is mechanically necessary and
fictionally hollow. **The cars come back around.** A grenade turns up on a forecourt two
streets over, or in the yard you buy from, and word travels with it.

### Standing is throughput, and that IS the ratchet fix

    presence(b) = basePresence(b) * reputationFlowFactor(reputation, b) * seasonFactor(b, week)

**Unlocks stay ratcheted** — confiscating a tool the player bought feels arbitrary. What
standing buys is **flow**: how many buyers appear, how good they are, and what the yard holds
for you. That applies at every level forever, so standing never stops mattering.
`TODO.md`'s ratchet entry closes against this section.

**The on-ramp back.** "Dodgy early, honest late" is a corridor with a locked door unless
standing recovers. It does, through the same flow model: clean and concours sales raise it
continuously, with no floor that traps a player and no penalty box. The rate wants signing
alongside the lemon cost.

## 6. The dodgy trade, and the exits

> **Cheap in, grimy work, certain out, and out of the bay fast.**

Rough cars need MORE hours, not fewer. The trade wins the bay and the input price, never the
hour. Path 3 is the heaviest labour in the table and the lightest on space.

**In: the scrapyard, gated on favour, never on disgrace.** Gating it on low public standing
would reward a player for deliberately selling lemons to tank their own reputation. **Dodgy
sales build favour; favour opens the yard.** The honest player still arrives later through
campaign or cash.

**Harvesting is the yard's routing puzzle, scored.** Parts pulled on a good route cost less
than book, on a range of **0.70 to 0.90** rather than a flat factor. It is the dodgy trade's
skill axis, mirroring auction diagnosis on the honest side, and it **charges the visit day**.

### The four exits, properly spaced

| exit | pays | speed | takes | notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shop front / free ads / magazine / meet | taste | slow | anything listable | the listed economy |
| trade network | **0.87** | fast | **runners only** | the neutral floor |
| the fixer | **0.80** | instant | **literally anything** | pays the rest in favour; **capped monthly** |
| export container | **0.95, paid on departure** | **fast bay, slow cash** | batch of 3 to 4 | taste-, mileage- and shaken-blind |

**The trade network requires a runner.** That is what makes the fixer's monopoly real rather
than rhetorical, and it puts a genuine sub-decision inside Path 3: spend the labour to make it
run and reach the better price, or take the fixer's worse one today.

**The fixer's appetite is capped per month, resetting with the container.** An uncapped buyer
of last resort is an infinite sink; a capped one makes favour scarce and him a person rather
than a vending machine.

**Calling in a favour costs slots and labour on HIS schedule** — a bay for a week, your
mechanic tomorrow. Always refusable, visibly brewing, never a fail state.

**Export never loses the car.** Delay, a bounded arrival haircut, or a fee-released hold, all
seeded at loading. The game has no random catastrophic loss anywhere and export should not
introduce the first.

**The free ads paper**, unmentioned for three versions, earns its place by **widening
presence**: cheap, low ceiling, but it puts an ordinary car in front of more archetypes than
the shop front does. It is the volume channel for common metal. **If that niche is not wanted,
cut it** rather than leave a channel nobody has a reason to choose.

## 7. Time, space and rhythm

**Rent and wages are flat, which is why they are noise.** Waiting stops being free the moment
it competes with something. **And if waiting is always bad, dump-fast dominates and the
degenerate strategy has merely moved.**

**1. Space.** A car holds a slot from purchase to sale. **Forecourt and covered storage are
different slots**: viewings happen at the forecourt, so a listed car needs one, while storage
is cheaper and holds stock you are not selling yet. The COMPOSITION of your space becomes a
decision. Authentic, too: **shako shōmei** means you must prove you have somewhere to put a car
before you may own it.

**2. Weekly rhythm.** Auction day, the weekend meet, wages on Friday, the container's monthly
departure, and a catalogue that rotates with good lots that appear once.

**3. Staleness and the quality slide**, on the normalised clock, §4.

**4. Heat drift under held cars**, which is what makes waiting *sometimes right* and therefore
stops dump-fast dominating.

**Held back:** floorplan financing. A difficulty option, not baseline.

**What the table says.**

**The repair loop keeps its dignity.** Path 0 returns 60,147 on 360,000 committed, in under
eight days, at an expected quality of 0.976. Three systems now nibble at it — offer quality,
staleness, slot cost — and it remains comfortably profitable. **That invariant is
load-bearing: a well-bought, well-repaired, promptly-sold ordinary car must always pay.**
Everything else here is a deviation from that baseline, and deviations only feel like choices
if the baseline feels safe.

**Each path is the extreme of exactly one constraint.**

- **Path 4, restoration, is the best per hour** at 10,531 a slot and the largest absolute
  profit at 273,813. It is also 30 days and 26 slots: the endgame business, unreachable
  early because it needs the capital, the tools and the bays.
- **Path 3, salvage, is the worst per hour and by far the best per bay** at 112,175 a
  slot-week. Grimy, fast to clear, small money.
- **Path 1, the coherent build, is the space-hungry middle.** More absolute profit than the
  flip, less per bay, because one buyer takes three weeks to find.
- **Path 0 is the balanced baseline.**

**Harvesting earns its lever.** 95,525 against 80,125 on the same car, at the cost of a visit
day and a labour slot. It gives "cheap in" a mechanism and self-caps on what the yard holds.

**Path 2 remains a warning, not a business.** 63,715 if the stancer turns up after a month,
against a loss of 92,539 if he does not.

## 11. Period fidelity

**Shaken** is at **three years from new, then every two years**. Months remaining is a value
and liquidity modifier; a non-compliant build needs a compliance bill or a channel that does
not care. **It cuts both ways: a car near expiry is cheap at auction**, so months-remaining is
a buying skill, not only a selling tax, and the player can run the arbitrage the export trade
actually ran.

**Mileage.** Steeper domestic curve than today's 0.925 at 90,000 km, paired with the
mileage-blind export.

**Era events on heat.** Gran Turismo in 1997-98, the R34 launch pushing the R33 down, Best
Motoring features, kei sports discontinuations. **Seasonal presence**: racers in season,
stancers thin in winter.

## 13. Whole-game integration

**1. One market, two sides.** Auction generation and NPC bidding must consume the **same
valuation stack** as the player's sale, run with noise and hidden information. Any gap between
two valuation functions is free money, and the dominant strategy becomes arbitraging our own
inconsistency. **The player's edge must be knowledge, never a better formula.** This is a prose
ruling and it should be signed before any number.

**2. Stat gaps become economic bugs the moment the economy consumes every stat.** Style's stock
cap of 20 prices a 2000GT and a delivery van identically, which is harmless today and becomes a
pricing defect the instant any archetype targets looks. **Author per-car style baselines, or
formally redefine the stat as custom style only, before buyer targets are authored.** And
absolute power targets would lock entry cars out of premium channels: the answer is a
**kei-specialist archetype** — the Cappuccino, Beat and AZ-1 scene was real — not tier-relative
targets, which would make a Wagon R and a Supra equally impressive.

**3. Reputation architecture.** Public standing is a continuous throughput dial; underworld
favour is the second ledger. Both now exist. The **on-ramp back** is specced in §5.

**4. The decade clock.** The campaign runs ten years against static book values. **Keyframe
each car's value at 1995, 2000 and 2005**, with heat as noise on top. Era events get a spine,
covered storage becomes strategic, and decade-scale speculation is the strongest
"economy exists despite you" lever available per unit of cost.

**5. Seed everything.** Offers, lowball draws, lemon rolls, customs friction, harvest routes:
per car and per day, extending the auction philosophy economy-wide. **The difference between an
entity and a slot machine, and miserable to retrofit.** `tools/sale-value/model.mjs` is already
written this way.

**6. The tripartite law** is stated in §2.

**7. Path 4 exists** and is worked in §9.
